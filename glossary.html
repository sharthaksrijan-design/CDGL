Glossary

A quick reference to terms used throughout this guide. Definitions are concise; follow the linked chapters for mechanisms and examples.

A–Z terms

Allele

One of the alternative forms of a genetic sequence at a particular locus.

Anticodon

A three-nucleotide sequence in transfer RNA that pairs with a complementary messenger-RNA codon during translation.

Base

A nitrogen-containing component of a nucleotide. A, C, G, and T occur in DNA; RNA uses U in place of T.

Chromatin

The DNA, histone proteins, and associated molecules that package and regulate eukaryotic genomes.

Codon

A three-nucleotide sequence in messenger RNA that specifies an amino acid or a translation stop signal.

Complementary strand

A nucleic-acid strand whose bases pair with those of another strand according to base-pairing rules.

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid)

A nucleic acid that stores genetic sequence information in most cellular organisms.

Epigenetics

The study of molecular states that influence gene activity or chromatin without changing the underlying DNA sequence; persistence and inheritance vary by context.

Exon

A region retained in a mature RNA after splicing; in protein-coding genes, exons may include both coding and untranslated sequence.

Gene

A genomic region that contributes to a functional product, such as an RNA or a protein, together with the regulatory context needed for its expression.

Genome

The complete set of genetic material in an organism, cell, or virus.

Genotype

The genetic makeup of an organism or the alleles present at one or more loci.

Intron

A region removed from a precursor RNA during splicing in many eukaryotic genes.

Messenger RNA (mRNA)

An RNA molecule that carries sequence information used by ribosomes to synthesize a protein.

Mutation

A lasting change in nucleotide sequence. It is distinct from DNA damage, which may be repaired before becoming a mutation.

Nucleic acid

A DNA or RNA polymer made from nucleotide units.

Nucleotide

A nucleic-acid building block containing a sugar, phosphate group, and nitrogenous base.

Phenotype

An observable characteristic resulting from genetic, developmental, and environmental influences.

Polymerase

An enzyme that synthesizes a nucleic-acid strand using a template or other molecular instructions.

Promoter

A DNA region where transcription machinery assembles to initiate transcription.

Reading frame

The grouping of messenger-RNA nucleotides into successive, non-overlapping codons during translation.

Replication

The copying of a nucleic-acid genome or molecule to produce a new nucleic-acid strand or genome.

Ribosome

A ribonucleoprotein complex that reads messenger RNA and catalyzes protein synthesis.

RNA (ribonucleic acid)

A nucleic acid involved in information transfer, catalysis, structure, regulation, and—in some viruses—genome storage.

Splicing

The processing of a precursor RNA by removing introns and joining retained regions.

Template

A nucleic-acid strand used to guide synthesis of a complementary strand, or a molecule that promotes a corresponding structural state in another molecule.

Transcription

The synthesis of RNA using a DNA template, or in some systems an RNA template.

Translation

The synthesis of a protein by decoding messenger-RNA codons at a ribosome.

Variant

A sequence difference observed among individuals or samples; its effect may be neutral, harmful, beneficial, or uncertain depending on context.

Common abbreviations

  • ATP — adenosine triphosphate
  • CRISPR — clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats
  • DNA — deoxyribonucleic acid
  • mRNA — messenger RNA
  • RNA — ribonucleic acid
  • RNAi — RNA interference
  • RT — reverse transcription or reverse transcriptase, depending on context
  • RT-PCR — reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction
  • tRNA — transfer RNA
