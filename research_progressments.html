Research & reference

Research progress

A selection of milestones shows how the understanding of biological information flow has developed, from identifying DNA as a hereditary molecule to modern sequencing, editing, and structural prediction.

How to read this timeline

This is a concise guide to selected discoveries and technologies, not a complete history of molecular biology. Dates refer to publication or official milestones when practical. The history chapter gives additional context for foundational experiments; current research is summarized in open questions.

Selected milestones

1944 — DNA identified as the transforming principle

Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty presented evidence that DNA carried the activity responsible for transforming bacterial traits. The work helped establish DNA as genetic material. [Avery et al., 1944]

1953 — A structural model for DNA

Watson and Crick proposed a double-helical model that accounted for complementary base pairing and suggested a copying mechanism. The model became a foundation for explaining DNA replication, though it was built on evidence from many researchers. [Watson & Crick, 1953]

1961 — Messenger RNA and the genetic code

Experiments connected RNA sequence to protein synthesis, and the genetic code was progressively deciphered. This work provided a molecular basis for understanding how nucleotide sequences specify amino-acid sequences. [Nirenberg & Matthaei, 1961]

1970–1971 — Reverse transcription and the Baltimore classification

The discovery of reverse transcriptase established RNA-to-DNA copying as a biological pathway. Baltimore's later classification organized viruses according to how their genomes produce messenger RNA, making diverse viral strategies easier to compare. [Baltimore, 1970; Baltimore, 1971]

1990–2003 — Human Genome Project

The international Human Genome Project began in October 1990 and was declared complete in April 2003. It produced a reference sequence and advanced the technologies and collaborative methods that underpin modern genomics. [NHGRI: Human Genome Project]

2020 — mRNA vaccines used at population scale

COVID-19 vaccines demonstrated the clinical use of lipid nanoparticle-delivered mRNA to instruct cells to produce an antigen. This approach depends on RNA design, delivery, and immune biology; it does not alter a recipient's genome. [Polack et al., 2020]

2023 — First FDA-approved CRISPR/Cas9 therapy

The FDA approved Casgevy for eligible patients with sickle cell disease, a milestone in the clinical use of ex vivo genome editing. The treatment changes gene regulation in a patient's blood stem cells to increase fetal hemoglobin. [FDA, 2023]

2024 — Expanded biomolecular structure prediction

AlphaFold 3 extended machine-learning structure prediction to complexes involving proteins, nucleic acids, and other biomolecules. Predictions can guide experiments, but they are not a substitute for experimental validation and do not resolve every question about molecular dynamics or cellular function. [Abramson et al., 2024]

Recent developments

Reviewed November 2026. Long-read sequencing is improving the ability to study sequence variation and epigenetic marks across long DNA molecules. CRISPR-based functional screens are extending from cultured cells into more complex biological settings. RNA delivery and editing remain active areas, with ongoing work to improve tissue targeting, effectiveness, and safety. These topics are developing quickly; the linked reviews on the research snapshot provide recent context.

Sources and further reading

Links in the entries lead to primary papers or authoritative organizations where available. Dates and descriptions are intended as an accessible orientation; individual discoveries often build on work by many laboratories and should not be attributed to one paper alone.
