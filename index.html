A guided tour of molecular biology

The Central Dogma of Life

Explore how cells store, copy, and use biological information, from DNA and RNA to proteins and beyond.

Start with the big picture

Living systems preserve and use molecular information in many ways. The central dogma offers a starting map: DNA can be copied, transcribed into RNA, and—in the case of protein-coding messages—translated into protein. This guide follows those processes and examines the regulation, exceptions, and consequences that make information flow more than a simple diagram.

Begin with the introduction →

Follow the core processes

  • Replication — how genetic material is copied before cell division or genome propagation.
  • Transcription — how RNA is synthesized from a DNA template.
  • Translation — how ribosomes decode messenger RNA to make proteins.

Explore the wider picture

Once the core pathways are clear, continue into gene regulation, mutations and repair, and epigenetics to see how molecular information is controlled and changed. The extensions chapters cover reverse transcription, RNA replication, and viral genome strategies.
